# Means-restriction family: source verification packet

**Status:** model/source verified; **not expert gold**.

This document records a second-pass source check for the four-paper means-restriction family. It is intended to make human review faster and auditable. It does not replace independent domain-expert validation.

## 1. CSRP-REAL-004 — Hong Kong short-term intervention

**Paper:** Yip et al. *Restricting the means of suicide by charcoal burning*. British Journal of Psychiatry (2010).

**Source checked:** Cambridge Core article and PDF.

**Key source facts to verify in expert review**
- Intervention: charcoal removed from open shelves in major retail outlets in Tuen Mun for 12 months; Yuen Long was the control district.
- Charcoal-burning suicide rate in Tuen Mun: **4.3 → 2.0 per 100,000**, a **53.5% reduction**.
- Control district charcoal-burning rate: **3.0 → 4.3 per 100,000**.
- All-method rate in Tuen Mun: **17.9 → 12.2 per 100,000**.
- The report states that no significant increase in other suicide methods was observed.

- DOI: 10.1192/bjp.bp.109.065185

## 2. V03-MEANS-002 — New Taipei short-term intervention

**Paper:** Chen et al. *Assessing the Efficacy of Restricting Access to Barbecue Charcoal for Suicide Prevention in Taiwan: A Community-Based Intervention Trial*. PLoS ONE (2015).

**Source checked:** PubMed abstract and open PMC article.

**Key source facts**
- Quasi-experimental comparison of New Taipei City with Taipei City and Kaohsiung City.
- Intervention started 1 May 2012 and moved charcoal from open shelves to locked storage in major retail stores.
- Estimated charcoal-burning suicide reduction: **37% (95% CI 17–50%)** before secular-trend adjustment.
- Secular-trend-adjusted reduction: **30% (95% CI 14–44%)**.
- No compensatory rise in non-charcoal-burning suicide was observed.
- Simulation estimated **91 lives saved (95% CI 55–128)** during the 20-month intervention.

**Primary source**
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26305374/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4549118/
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0133809

## 3. V03-MEANS-003 — New Taipei long-term follow-up

**Paper:** Chang et al. *Assessing the effect of restricting access to barbecue charcoal for suicide prevention in New Taipei City, Taiwan: A controlled interrupted time series analysis*. Journal of Affective Disorders (2021).

**Source checked:** PubMed abstract and HKU Scholars Hub metadata.

**Key source facts**
- Controlled interrupted time-series analysis using quarterly age-standardised suicide rates from 2007–2017.
- No clear post-intervention step difference for charcoal-burning suicide: **−0.336 per 100,000 (95% CI −1.173 to 0.502)** intervention minus comparison.
- No clear step difference for overall suicide: **−0.270 (95% CI −1.844 to 1.303)**.
- No clear trend-change difference for charcoal-burning suicide: **0.007 (95% CI −0.055 to 0.069)**.
- Authors conclude that the programme showed **no effect over the five years after implementation**.
- Important limitations: no legislative enforcement and restriction applied only to a subset of stores.

**Primary source**
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33601720/
- https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/306753
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2020.12.147

## 4. V03-MEANS-004 — Regional trend context

**Paper:** Chang et al. *Regional changes in charcoal-burning suicide rates in East/Southeast Asia from 1995 to 2011: a time trend analysis*. PLoS Medicine (2014).

**Source checked:** PubMed abstract and open PMC article.

**Key source facts**
- Regional time-trend study, not an intervention evaluation.
- Charcoal-burning suicide increased markedly in several East/Southeast Asian settings, with variation in timing and scale.
- In 2011, charcoal burning accounted for different proportions of all suicides across Hong Kong, Taiwan, Japan, the Republic of Korea and Singapore.
- Association with changes in overall suicide was not consistent across all settings.
- Coding differences across countries are an explicit limitation.

**Primary source**
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24691071/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3972087/
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pmed.1001622

## Why this family is scientifically useful

This family contains three distinct comparison modes:

1. **Short-term replication across jurisdictions** — Hong Kong 2010 vs New Taipei 2015.
2. **Short-term vs long-term within the same policy context** — New Taipei 2015 vs New Taipei 2021.
3. **Intervention evidence vs contextual epidemiology** — New Taipei/Hong Kong intervention studies vs regional trend analysis.

A strong reasoning system should not collapse all directional differences into a binary contradiction label.
