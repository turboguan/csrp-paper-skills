# Relation-label guide

The benchmark labels relations between **claims**, not entire papers.

## SUPPORTS

Use when two claims address a sufficiently similar scientific estimand and point in a compatible direction.

Differences in jurisdiction alone do not automatically prevent SUPPORTS, but reviewers should record transferability limits.

## CONTRADICTS

Use only when claims are sufficiently comparable in population, intervention/exposure, outcome, method/estimand and timeframe, yet make materially incompatible assertions.

Do **not** use CONTRADICTS simply because one study is beneficial and another is null.

## QUALIFIES

Use when one claim changes the scope, strength or conditions under which another should be interpreted.

Typical reasons:
- short-term vs long-term follow-up;
- different implementation fidelity;
- subgroup vs overall population;
- different but related methods;
- different settings that affect generalizability.

## NOT_COMPARABLE

Use when the claims answer materially different scientific questions or estimate different quantities.

Examples:
- regional epidemiological trend vs direct intervention effect;
- knowledge/attitude outcome vs suicide mortality when no bridging claim is made.

## UNRELATED

Use when there is no meaningful evidential relation relevant to the benchmark question.

## Adjudication rule

Reviewers should first decide whether the claims address the same or a nested estimand. Direction is considered only after comparability.

The model-suggested label must be hidden from reviewers during independent labeling.
