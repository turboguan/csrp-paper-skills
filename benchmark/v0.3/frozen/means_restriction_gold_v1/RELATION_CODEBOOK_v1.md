# Relation codebook v1

The object being labeled is a **claim pair**, not a paper pair.

## SUPPORTS

Use when the claims address the same or sufficiently nested scientific estimand and their conclusions are compatible.

Support does not require identical effect sizes or identical jurisdictions.

## CONTRADICTS

Use only when:
- population/setting are sufficiently comparable;
- intervention/exposure is sufficiently comparable;
- outcome/estimand is sufficiently comparable;
- method and timeframe do not explain the difference;
- the claims make materially incompatible assertions.

A beneficial result and a null result are **not automatically contradictory**.

## QUALIFIES

Use when one claim meaningfully changes the scope, duration, strength, transferability, or conditions under which another claim should be interpreted.

Common qualification mechanisms:
- short-term vs long-term follow-up;
- implementation fidelity/coverage;
- voluntary vs enforced implementation;
- subgroup vs overall population;
- different but related analytic designs;
- jurisdictional differences affecting external validity.

## NOT_COMPARABLE

Use when the claims answer materially different scientific questions or estimate different quantities.

Example: regional descriptive epidemiology versus a direct intervention-effect estimate.

## UNRELATED

Use when no meaningful evidential relation exists for the benchmark question.

## Labeling order

Reviewers should decide in this order:

1. Are the claims scientifically related?
2. Do they address the same or a nested estimand?
3. Are population, intervention/exposure, outcome, method and timeframe sufficiently comparable?
4. If comparable, are the conclusions compatible or incompatible?
5. If not fully comparable, does one nevertheless constrain/qualify interpretation of the other?

Do not begin with effect direction and work backward.
